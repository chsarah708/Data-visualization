--- Question 2: Audit of Honest vs. Distorted Graphics ---

• Honest Graphic: Chart C (Export earnings) is the ONLY honest graphic (Lie Factor = 1.000).
  Its bars originate from a true zero baseline, and ink length scales in strict 1:1 proportion
  to the 20.0% data increase ($25B -> $30B, ink 5.0cm -> 6.0cm).

• Distortions in the other four graphics:
  - Chart A (Corporate tax rate, LF = 75.000): Severe truncation of the baseline. Bars are drawn
    from a 29.6% baseline instead of zero, displaying only the tips. A modest 6.67% rate increase
    (30% -> 32%) is depicted as a 500.0% ink explosion (4mm -> 24mm), exaggerating reality 75-fold.
  - Chart B (Federal health budget, LF = 2.500): 2D area encoding of a 1D scalar. Sizing both
    height and width of a coin icon by 1.5x for a 50% budget increase (Rs 40B -> 60B) squares the visual
    surface area (1.5^2 = 2.25x ink, +125% graphic effect), exaggerating the increase 2.5 times.
  - Chart D (Air-pollution index, LF = 0.250): Compressed baseline / downward offset. A 50.0% rise
    in air pollution (100 -> 150) is encoded as only a 12.5% bar height increase (8.0cm -> 9.0cm),
    understating the pollution surge by a factor of 4 (LF = 0.25).
  - Chart E (City population, LF = 5.000): Bubble radius scaling. Encoding a 300.0% population
    growth (2M -> 8M, a 4x factor) by scaling bubble RADIUS 4x results in area scaling by 4^2 = 16x
    (+1500.0% ink area), exaggerating growth 5-fold.

--- Question 3: Dimensionality of Ink (ink_dimension=2) ---

• Graphics requiring ink_dimension=2: Chart B (coin icon) and Chart E (bubble graphic).
  Both represent data magnitudes using two-dimensional visual surface area rather than a 1D line/bar length.

• What goes wrong if left at ink_dimension=1:
  If ink_dimension is left at 1, the formula incorrectly assumes the reader's eye perceives only the 1D linear
  dimension (diameter or radius) rather than the 2D surface area (ink ~ r^2); for Chart B this would falsely
  report an honest Lie Factor of 1.00 (ignoring the 2.25x area expansion), and for Chart E it would report
  a Lie Factor of 1.00 instead of the true 5.00x visual area distortion.

--- Question 4: Understated Lie Factor (LF < 1) ---

• Why an understated chart is still a distortion:
  Visual integrity requires faithful proportionality in both directions; understating a magnitude
  lulls the viewer into false complacency by suppressing the true scale of growth, creating a visual
  perception that change is minor when it is substantial.

• Who benefits:
  Polluting industries, municipal regulators, or environmental agencies benefit from Chart D because
  understating a severe 50% spike in hazardous smog (100 -> 150 AQI) as an almost imperceptible bump (8cm -> 9cm)
  minimizes public outcry, evades regulatory scrutiny, and shields polluters from liability.
